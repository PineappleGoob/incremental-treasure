Great Big Hole is a 2d clicker with aspects of point and click games. With upgrades for every person, this is the best incremental game we’ve made so far. We started the project by working on the concept itself, while it started as a 3d game for most features, we quickly narrowed it down to 2d. After we settled on a concept, we began making the art. This art was simple, as the art in an incremental is not the most important part. Then we moved to coding, we split into two groups here. Actual code, and balancing for the upgrades as well as preparing upgrade ideas for the rest of the game. This pre-making of the upgrades allowed us to easily plug in the upgrades into the game, as all it was was a bunch of value.

Now, onto the gameplay itself. It is a simple incremental. You start off on this island, you can click certain places to go there, such as the hole. When you enter the hole, if you click you can mine downward, the mining speed starts slow. But they get increasingly faster as you buy more upgrades. Later you can even get different types of pickaxes, these pickaxes allow you to mine at absolutely uncharted speed. But some of you may be saying, how will I know how fast I’m going? Well, lucky for you, we have what we call the depthometer. It tells you exactly how many meters you’re going down a second. Next you’d go to the shopkeep. But the shopkeep is actually a blood altar. It takes shits as a currency. There are three currencies, shits, giggles, and bags o’ milk. All three of these have a specific shop. After buying an upgrade or two, you can mine with far greater efficiency. Now, exploring the map has many spoils to gain. There are many rooms, namely everyone’s favorite, the Random Ass Rainbow Room. This room is very bad for your eyes, it’s great. Now, we also have the ship. It’s just a ship with an expansive amount of items on it. We also have a room for Big Milker’s Milk shop. He sells Bags o’ milk for a high cost, they can be used to get a Canadian implant, which changes every bit of text on screen to be Canadian. It also allows you to be drugged by Maple Syrup every now and again, Maple Syrup is a massive buff for 20 seconds. But wait, there’s more. Because we also have the Kiddy pool. The Kiddy Pool is where people buy the Canadian implant. It’s also in outer space, why is it in outer space? Who knows. Now for the mining sequence, if I haven’t already went over it. Basically it starts when you click on this hole, it takes you to the mining zone. In the mining zone you can either click or jump. Jumping has a long cooldown but breaks more than the default shovel. But there’s more! You are able to hire F.O.R.B. to help you mine faster, they bounce around your screen and mine with you, but they get distracted easily. To put them back to work you have to click them. 

Struggles. 
	We struggled a lot when it came to the creation of upgrades. Most of these upgrades were cut, as they were thought of when we thought it was a 3d game. We also struggled to allow everyone to get hours coding, as none of us had ever used gamemaker and we had to learn the engine, as well as not everyone here knows how to code. Now, because not all of us have ever coded before, we had to make tasks that could actually do, this includes many things, including art and documentation. This is an example of what we forced them to do. Now, while all those troubles seem mild, the main trouble we had was how we all saw the game in a different way, one of us looked at it like it was a clicker, one of us a mining simulator, one of us like a psychopath. Whilst we worked hard, we had to remake many things because our ideas of the game were not well communicated between each other. We also had the problem that the artist had trouble with many of the things to make in this type of game, namely struggling with making large things, such as sky and ground, and worse, he couldn’t make a good person, that is why there is a Waldo head and not a Waldo body. You may be wondering ‘where’s Waldo?’. Yeah, Waldo is hiding, you have to find him yourself. 

How we thought of things.
We started by brainstorming ideas for the upgrades, this was simple at first, like pick upgrade, rope length, rope attention span, so on and so forth. Now, after that we thought it’d be funny to label them with a name, so the pick upgrade at the time was called give me the scoop, because it was a shovel at the time. We had many upgrade names. We had caffeine overdose, The Namekian Special, Banana Boost, Big Black Pack, Mega Filler, gayness, Canadian implant, and FORB. While all of these are good, we had to limit them a little for two reasons, lack of time and some just didn't fit the part. Now we get to the shopkeeps. Basically what happened for them, is the artist couldn’t make a good humanoid at the time, so they made a blood altar, this blood altar became the main shop. Next they made Waldo, Waldo was made initially as a joke in an area, but then it was thought that he’d be a good shopkeeper, maybe he’d even say something funny like, ‘oh hey, ya found me’ or something along those lines. Now, to explain the currencies. We named the currencies to be funny, because that’s just what we should do, a fun game requires a funny thought. If there are no gags or humorise things how would we keep the audience on their toes for these jokes. We thought of shits and giggles as currencies by talking a lot, we talked about how it’s all shits and giggles until someone giggles and shits, then I thought what if we make that the currency. And when no one stopped me, I made it the official currency. By the time they realized it was already too late for them, it would forever be shits and giggles. Now, as for bags o’ milk, bags o’ milk was a currency idea given by the Canadian, and I thought, oh sure, it’d be funny. And so I made it that the bags o’ milk would only give upgrades that affect the Canadian part of the game. As such the Canadian implant became a thing. Later we also made it so the Bags O’ Milk could be opened to become bags. I thought it’d be really funny, because from a bag of milk, you open it and all the milk falls to the floor. Like, imagine your trying to buy the Canadian implant, and you open a bag o’ milk and you lose your chance at buying the Canadian implant because of that. I thought it’d be really funny to watch someone lose their joy for a moment when they realize what they’ve done. We also wanted to have time spent in the Random Ass Rainbow Room, but we never got to it because the only upgrade we thought of for it was Gayness, which would’ve been really difficult to add to our current game at the time. 

Art explanation.

At first I started with drawing the map, it was very simple at the time, I hadn’t yet thought of it as an island, then I started to refine it, it took me many attempts, I had to change the angles, I had to remake the palm tree multiple times. The problem I had was that I couldn’t get the leaves just as I liked them, so I kept remaking them, time after time I had to erase and redraw it and hope it looked better. Now, I’ve never really been good at making trees or art pallets for games like these, so I expected a lot of trouble. Now, not only did I pick a color pallet too bright, as the coders had explained in great depth, but I also had some of my artwork full on ignore as it was too low quality, which to be fair, it did look quite bad, and I couldn’t draw some of these things at all. The map I struggled most with because I drew it as I saw it in my mind, like an empty street, but the problem with that is the coders saw the game as an island, so they said I should make it an island. So I did. I also struggled with just thinking of what to make. After making and finishing some things I struggled to continue making things. Like when I finished one thing I would get art block and not know what more to make, it wasn’t helpful how the list I was given for sprites to make was less than he actually wanted, so I had to guess for the majority of the sprites I made. I also struggled with consistency and preparing myself for the future. I waited more than half the week before starting this project. This forced me to work more and more each day, which is terrible for me because I get art block easily.
